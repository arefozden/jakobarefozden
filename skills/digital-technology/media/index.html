LESSON 01

Production workflow

Understand

Live production combines capture, audio, switching, encoding, distribution and monitoring. A production plan defines roles, scene order, equipment and contingency actions.

Try it yourself

Create a running order for a fictional school broadcast.

Knowledge check: Why monitor the outgoing stream?

To detect problems viewers experience.

LESSON 02

Camera audio and lighting

Understand

Frame shots intentionally, use stable exposure and white balance, position microphones close to sources and monitor levels. Avoid clipping, distracting backgrounds and unsafe cables.

Try it yourself

Record a short offline practice scene and review sound and lighting.

Knowledge check: What is clipping?

Signal peaks exceeding the available recording range.

LESSON 03

Multicamera and OBS

Understand

A video mixer or software such as OBS combines sources into scenes. Preview before switching where supported. Use transitions and graphics consistently, and check audio synchronization.

Try it yourself

Build an offline scene collection with a title card and two sources.

Knowledge check: What is a scene?

A configured combination of sources.

LESSON 04

Streaming quality

Understand

Resolution, frame rate, codec and bitrate affect quality and bandwidth. At 4 Mbps video plus 128 kbps audio, one hour transfers about 1.86 GB before overhead. Leave upload headroom and test a private stream.

Try it yourself

Estimate data use and compare two bitrate settings.

Knowledge check: Why leave upload headroom?

To tolerate overhead and changing network conditions.

LESSON 05

Rights and contingency

Understand

Obtain appropriate permissions, respect copyright and privacy, and plan moderation. Keep a fallback recording and clear responsibilities for stopping a broadcast if there is a problem.

Try it yourself

Write a preflight checklist for a school event.

Knowledge check: Why keep a local recording?

It may preserve the event if the live connection fails.
